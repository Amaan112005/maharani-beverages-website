## The trust model for a drinking-water purchase

Trust = **verifiability × proximity × consistency** [H synthesised from 02-consumer research].
A water buyer can't test the product before purchase — so they audit the *supplier*: licence (authority), location (proximity/realness), process (competence), and responsiveness (accountability). Every trust asset must answer one of those four audits.

## Message architecture (brand voice rules)

1. **Prove, don't claim** — every quality statement pairs with its evidence (licence number, test reference, process detail). No "purest/safest/best" language anywhere (also a legal-prudence rule).
2. **Local specificity beats adjectives** — "Ranihati plant, daily routes through Sector V" outperforms "premium quality" [H, consistent with persona research].
3. **Regime-aware accuracy** — quality claims reference the current FSSAI testing regime (post-Jan-2026), not the retired certification model (Phase-1 verified wording).
4. **Honest uncertainty** — the unconfirmed-serviceability pattern (pincode checker) is the voice model: say what's true, offer the next step.

## Publication gates (binding)

No trust asset ships without: (a) source document in the owner register (confirmed status), (b) G3 review where regulatory, (c) a named page destination in the IA map. The lab library is the flagship — publish monthly summaries, dated, with lab name and parameters — **only from real reports**.
