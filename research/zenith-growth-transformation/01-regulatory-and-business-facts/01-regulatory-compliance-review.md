## 1. The verified regulatory timeline

| Date | Instrument | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **17 Oct 2024** | FSSAI Gazette notification omitting sub-regulation 2.3.14, clauses 17 & 18 of the Food Safety and Standards (Prohibition and Restriction on Sales) Regulations, 2011 | **Mandatory BIS certification for packaged drinking water (PDW) and mineral water (MW) was withdrawn.** Those clauses were the legal basis of compulsion. |
| **29 Nov 2024** | FSSAI classification order | PDW/MW reclassified as a **high-risk food category** → annual third-party audit, stricter inspection regime for licensed manufacturers. |
| **17 Dec 2025** | FSSAI enforcement order/advisory | **Compulsory Scheme of Testing** notified for all licensed PDW/MW manufacturers, replacing the certification model. |
| **1 Jan 2026** | Scheme of Testing in force | **Monthly microbiological testing of finished product** (coliform, faecal streptococci, *S. aureus*, *P. aeruginosa*, yeast & mould, *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Vibrio*) + **source-water testing at FSSAI-notified NABL-accredited labs** + batch-wise record keeping. Compliance is demonstrated by **continuous lab records, not a certificate**. |

## 2. What is true today (plain language)

1. **An Indian Standard still exists:** IS 14543 remains the technical specification PDW is judged against (product standard under the FSSAI regime). **[F]**
2. **BIS certification is now voluntary** — available as a trust asset, not a legal precondition to sell. **[F]**
3. **FSSAI licensing remains mandatory**, and PDW manufacture now sits in the **high-risk category** (annual third-party audit). **[F]**
4. **Mandatory testing obligations exist and are new:** monthly finished-product microbiological testing + source-water testing at NABL-notified labs, from 1 Jan 2026. **[F]**
5. **Distributor/marketer vs manufacturer:** the obligations above attach to the *licensed manufacturer*. A brand owner/marketer who does not manufacture has a different (lighter) obligation set, but every product sold under its brand must carry an FSSAI licence number and the manufacturer of record must meet the scheme. Zenith manufactures at its Ranihati plant (per site claims) → **the manufacturer obligations apply to Zenith directly** [F — plant claim from site; licence number 12825999000938 verifiable on FSSAI's FoSCoS portal].

## 3. R1 correction (record of error, per programme honesty standards)

The master research package (deliverable 01, Risk R1) stated: *"the site's FAQ says BIS 'no longer mandatory'; multiple 2026 industry sources say IS 14543 certification is legally required to SELL packaged drinking water — one of these is wrong."* **The investigation finds the site was right and the research package's framing was wrong:** the industry sources it cited (aimtechindia, elitasrcs, registerkaro) describe the pre-Oct-2024 regime or conflate voluntary BIS with mandatory; the 2025-2026 sources above are consistent and specific. The R1 escalation was nevertheless the correct process — this is exactly the kind of claim that must be verified, and it now has been, against primary instruments.

## 4. Consequences and opportunities

### A. No correction needed — 7 files carry the accurate claim
The "BIS no longer mandatory; FSSAI's testing scheme applies" FAQ (packaged-drinking-water-rajarhat/new-town/dum-dum/garia + generated schema FAQs, 7 file-claims) **stands verified**. [F]

### B. Two accuracy improvements (owner-gated, G3 review)
1. **Precision wording upgrade [H→owner]:** the claim could be made stronger and more precise: *"BIS certification is voluntary since 17 Oct 2024 (FSSAI Gazette). Zenith meets IS 14543 product standards and tests under FSSAI's mandatory Scheme of Testing (monthly, NABL-notified labs)."* — only publish the Zenith-specific half if monthly NABL testing is actually happening **[U — owner must confirm]**.
2. **Trust asset decision [owner — D-6]:** under the new regime, compliance is *lab-record-based*. Publishing genuine monthly test summaries converts a legal obligation into the market's strongest differentiator (national brands don't; local rivals can't). This is deliverable P1-3 of the roadmap, now with a regulatory rationale.
