20 Litre Water Can vs Jar vs Bottle: What's the Difference?

Quick answer: a "water can", a "water jar", and a "20 litre bottle" usually mean the same thing — a 20-litre refillable container of packaged drinking water. "Can" is simply the everyday word in most of South and East India (including Kolkata), while "jar" is the word suppliers and the industry use. The real differences that matter are dispenser compatibility and size variants, covered below.

The differences that actually matter

1. Dispenser compatibility

Standard 20 litre jars are made to fit water dispensers, but neck styles differ (wide-mouth vs bubble-top). If the jar is for a dispenser, say so when ordering — your supplier should match the neck type.

2. Retail bottles vs jars

Small bottles (250ml–2L) are single-use retail packs; the 20 litre jar is a returnable, refillable container. For offices and households with a dispenser, the jar is the economical standard; for events and guests, small bottles are convenient.

3. What is inside

Whatever the container is called, the water must meet the same legal standard: packaged drinking water sold in India requires an FSSAI licence, and since 1 January 2026 manufacturers must test monthly under FSSAI's Scheme of Testing. Check the label, not the nickname — our label guide shows the seven checks.

4. Deposit and exchange

Returnable jars usually involve a refundable deposit and an exchange-for-exchange swap on delivery. Terms vary by supplier — ask before your first order.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a water can the same as a water jar?

Yes, in everyday usage they are the same product: a 20-litre refillable container of packaged drinking water. "Can" is common in South and East India; "jar" is the supplier term.

Does a 20 litre jar fit a standard water dispenser?

Standard 20 litre jars are made for dispensers. Check the neck type (wide-mouth or bubble-top) and tell your supplier it is for dispenser use.

What should I check when ordering a 20 litre water can?

The FSSAI licence number, batch code, seal integrity, and delivery terms. Ask about the jar deposit and exchange policy before the first order.

Bottle vs jar: which should my office choose?

For staffed desks, small bottles are convenient but cost more per litre. For pantries and dispenser points, the 20 litre jar is the office standard.
