How to Read a Packaged Water Bottle Label in India: 7 Checks Before You Drink

Quick answer: a legal packaged drinking water label in India must show the FSSAI licence number, net quantity, batch or lot number, manufacturing date, MRP, the manufacturer's name and address, and customer care details. If any of these is missing or looks tampered with, do not drink the water.

By Maharani Beverages LLP — Quality & Compliance Team · Published 31 October 2026 · Sources: FSSAI Gazette notification (17 Oct 2024), FSSAI Scheme of Testing order (17 Dec 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026), FSS (Packaging & Labelling) Regulations 2011

You cannot test water quality with your eyes — but you can audit the bottle. Indian food law requires every packaged drinking water label to carry specific information, and each line tells you something about whether the water inside is trustworthy. Here are the seven checks, in the order that matters most.

1. The FSSAI licence number

This is the single most important line on the label. Packaged drinking water is a food product, and every brand selling it in India must hold an FSSAI licence and print its number on the pack. Check the format (a 14-digit number), then verify it on FSSAI's FoSCoS portal — the licence should show the manufacturer's legal name, address, and validity. Our step-by-step verification checklist walks through exactly where to click.

2. Batch code and date

A genuine manufacturer tracks every batch. Look for a batch or lot number and a manufacturing/packing date, usually printed or laser-marked near the cap or base. No batch code means no traceability — and no way the maker could recall that water if a problem emerged.

3. The standard reference

Labels commonly reference IS 14543 — the Indian Standard that specifies quality parameters for packaged drinking water. One regulatory note worth knowing: mandatory BIS certification for packaged water was removed by FSSAI's Gazette notification of 17 October 2024. Since 1 January 2026, compliance is demonstrated through FSSAI's compulsory Scheme of Testing — monthly microbiological testing of finished product at notified NABL-accredited laboratories, plus source-water testing. An FSSAI licence remains mandatory, and IS 14543 remains the technical standard the water itself is judged against.

4. Net quantity and MRP

Net quantity (e.g., "500 ml") and the Maximum Retail Price must be printed on the pack — not only on a shop sticker. A fresh sticker over a printed price is a red flag.

5. Manufacturer traceability

The label must name the manufacturer or packer with a real address. "Bottled by" a marketing company with no plant address deserves a second look — find out who actually makes the water. 6. Seal and cap integrity

Check the cap seal before paying: it should be intact, tight, and unwrinkled. Tamper-evident rings that are already broken mean the bottle has been opened or refilled — a genuine safety risk, not a cosmetic issue.

7. Customer care details

A responsible brand prints a way to reach it — phone number, email, or both. If there is no contact path on the label, there is no one to hold accountable.

Red flags — any one of these is reason to walk away:
  • Missing, blurred, or unverifiable FSSAI number
  • No batch code or manufacturing date
  • Tampered seal, loose cap, or refilled appearance
  • MRP only on a shop sticker
  • No manufacturer name or address anywhere on the pack

Frequently Asked Questions

What must a packaged drinking water label show in India?

At minimum: the FSSAI licence number, net quantity, batch or lot number, manufacturing/packing date, MRP, the manufacturer's name and address, and customer care contact details.

Is the BIS ISI mark still required on water bottles in 2026?

No. FSSAI removed the mandatory BIS certification requirement through a Gazette notification on 17 October 2024. Since 1 January 2026, manufacturers must follow FSSAI's Scheme of Testing with monthly testing at notified NABL-accredited laboratories. An FSSAI licence remains mandatory.

How do I verify a water brand's FSSAI licence number?

Search the licence number on FSSAI's FoSCoS portal (foscos.fssai.gov.in). The licence should show the brand's legal name, address, and validity. Our verification checklist shows each step.

What are the red flags on a water bottle label?

Missing or unverifiable FSSAI number, no batch code or date, tampered seal or cap, MRP sticker over the printed price, and no manufacturer traceability. Any one of these is reason not to drink the water.
